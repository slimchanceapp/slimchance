Why the scale went up overnight

A Slim Chance guide · October 2026

You did everything right yesterday and the scale says you're up a kilo. It isn't fat. Here is what moves the scale from one morning to the next, roughly how much, and how to read your weight so a morning like this one stops mattering.

The short version. To gain a kilo of fat you'd need to eat about 7,000 kcal more than you burned, on top of everything else that day. Overnight jumps are water, food that hasn't left yet, and glycogen. They come back off within days. The number worth watching is your trend: a line through several weeks of mornings.

It can't be fat

A kilogram of body fat holds roughly 7,000 to 7,700 kcal of energy. A 1.5 kg jump overnight would need more than ten thousand extra calories eaten in a day. People don't do that by accident. What they do every day is drink, eat salt, eat carbohydrate, carry yesterday's dinner, and train, and each of those moves water.

Put two or three of these together, a salty Saturday dinner and Monday's first workout in a while, and a 1.5 kg jump is ordinary.

How to read your weight

Weigh yourself the same way most mornings: after the toilet, before food or drink. Then stop reading single mornings. Read the trend, a line drawn through the last few weeks of them. The trend barely moves for one heavy morning, and over a few weeks it shows what your body is actually doing.

A good trend also tells you how sure it is. Five weeks of mornings that scatter half a kilo either side of a slowly falling line can still say "down between 0.4 and 0.6 kg a week" with confidence. Two mornings can't say anything.
