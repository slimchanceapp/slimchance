How to read it. One morning says little: water, salt, a late meal and a hard workout move the scale more than a week of eating well. A week of mornings says a lot. The line through them is your trend, and the chance is how sure the numbers are that it's really heading your way, not just noise.

How it works

Your readings are fitted with a line that weighs recent mornings most, the same way the Slim Chance app does it every day. How much your mornings scatter around that line is your scale's noise. From the slope and the noise comes a range for your weekly rate, and from that range the chance in 10 that your weight is really moving the way you want. Fewer than about a week of mornings, or very noisy ones, and it tells you how many more it needs instead of guessing.

How many weigh-ins do I need?

About a week of mornings for a first answer, two to three weeks for a confident one. Weigh at the same time, after the bathroom and before food or drink, and keep every reading, high ones included. Skipping the bad mornings makes the trend look better than it is.

Why does my weight jump from day to day?

Water, mostly: salt, carbohydrate refilling your muscles' glycogen, a hard workout, the menstrual cycle, and food still on its way through. A kilo of fat holds about 7,000 kcal, so an overnight jump can't be fat. The guide why the scale went up overnight goes through each cause and how big it is.

What does "7 in 10" mean?

That given your readings, there's about a 70% chance your weight is really going your way, and a 30% chance the drop is noise. 9 or 10 in 10 is about as sure as a bathroom scale gets. A low number early on isn't bad news: it usually means the line is still forming.
