# One morning on the scale says almost nothing. A week says a lot.

*Substack draft, 11 Nov 2026. Link tag: `?from=substack`.*

You weigh yourself on Tuesday: down 0.6 kg. Wednesday: up 0.9. You ate the
same both days. Nothing you did explains it, and that's the point: nothing
you did caused it.

A body carries a few kilos of water that come and go. A salty dinner holds
some back for two or three days. A hard workout does too, while the muscles
repair. So does a late meal, a bad night's sleep, the time of the month.
Fat changes slowly, by grams a day; water changes by hundreds of grams
overnight. On any single morning, the water wins.

That's why the daily number feels like a verdict and behaves like a coin
toss. People quit on a Wednesday that meant nothing.

## What a week of mornings can tell you

Line up a week or two of mornings and the water starts to cancel out. Draw
the line that fits them best and its slope is the real direction: how fast
the weight is actually going, give or take.

The "give or take" matters as much as the slope. Ten noisy mornings aren't
ten independent facts: Tuesday's salty dinner is still in Wednesday's and
Thursday's numbers. Treat them as independent and you'll be far too sure
far too early. Allow for that carry-over and you get an honest answer to the
only question that matters: how likely is it that this is really going my
way?

## Ten coins instead of a number

Slim Chance shows that answer as ten coins. "9 in 10 it's coming off" means
that, given your own mornings, the trend is very likely heading toward your
goal. "5 in 10" means it's too early, or too flat, to tell, and that's fine
to hear in week one.

When the scale jumps overnight, the app says why from your own week: a big
day of eating, a hard session, the weekday it always jumps. The jump is
information about water, not a judgement about you.

## You don't have to log perfectly

Most calorie apps assume you log everything, forever. Almost nobody does.
Slim Chance learns your real budget from your scale and your log together:
if what you log runs a bit low every day, the target is set in those same
units, so the gap cancels out. A rough, consistent log is enough. It never
asks you to log more.

It's free, with no ads and no trackers, and it's open source. It runs in any
browser (on iPhone, add it to your home screen) and on Android.
